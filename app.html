MADM Decision Maker
ℹ

This tool supports group decision-making across two teams using Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA). Each team independently ranks criteria by importance and selects preferred alternatives, the combined view shows where teams agree and where they differ.

The goal is to help teams collaborate by making values and trade-offs explicit, decisions become better informed and more transparent.

Weight Exponent (p)
ℹ

Controls how strongly criteria priority order affects weights: wi = (n − ri + 1)p / Σ(n − rj + 1)p

p = 0: Equal weights
All criteria weighted equally; priority order ignored.
p = 0.5: Moderate priority
Weights decay gradually; top criteria somewhat favoured.
p = 1: Linear priority
Weights decrease linearly with rank.
Equal (0) Moderate (0.5) Linear (1)
Equal weights (p = 0)
MCDM Method
ℹ

When choosing between alternatives on multiple conflicting criteria, human judgment tends to unconsciously favour one or two criteria and apply trade-offs inconsistently, which means the same problem described in different words can produce different decisions. An MCDM algorithm removes that inconsistency: every criterion and weight is applied the same way to every alternative, making the ranking transparent and repeatable.

Different algorithms make slightly different assumptions about trade-offs, so comparing a few methods is more informative than relying on just one. See "About this method" below for what each method does.

ℹ
Consensus
One agreed-upon criteria order runs the MCDM jointly. Gives a single ranking but can mask real team disagreement.
Copeland
Each team ranks with their own weights. Results are combined by pairwise vote; the alternative preferred by more teams wins each head-to-head. "Table" view shows the full pairwise matrix.
Sensitivity
Interpolates between the two teams' weight vectors and re-runs the MCDM across the full range. Identifies "flip points" where the top alternative changes.

Method robustness

Axes = methods, lines = alternatives. Larger shape = consistently high score across methods.

Differences between method lines indicate alternatives whose ranking is sensitive to the choice of MCDM method. Where lines converge, the ranking is robust regardless of method.

Criteria Priority
?

Drag criteria up or down to reflect what matters most to your community. Rank 1 carries the most weight.

Priority only affects the results if you set the Weight Exponent above zero (at p = 0 all criteria are equal regardless of order).

Consider criteria that represent long-term or irreversible consequences: environmental degradation and ecosystem loss often unfold slowly but cannot easily be undone. Also consider whose voices may not be fully represented in the data: downstream communities, future generations, or groups with less political influence.

Ranked Alternatives
?

Use ★ to narrow a long list of alternatives down to the ones your team will actually consider. Checking Calculate selected only re-ranks just those, so scores reflect your shortlist rather than the full set.

Before finalising, re-examine the full list rankings to make sure you haven't overlooked an option that better serves your goals.
